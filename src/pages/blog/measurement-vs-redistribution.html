Zipli vs. measurement tools

Food waste measurement and food waste redistribution are not the same thing

Here is why it matters.

Ninja Fedy

19 August 2026 · 2 min read

There is a category confusion in the food waste technology market that is worth naming clearly.

Tools like Winnow and Leanpath measure food waste. They identify what is being discarded, in what quantities, at what times. They generate kitchen-level insights that help operations teams reduce over-production. They are valuable for exactly that purpose.

But they do not move the food.

Measurement without redistribution

Measurement without redistribution means the surplus identified by the tool still goes to bio-waste. The kitchen knows it is wasting less. But the food that is wasted is still wasted.

Zipli is a different kind of platform. The logging and the redistribution happen in the same system. When a kitchen logs surplus food, the donation is matched to a redistribution hub. The food moves to people who need it. The data trail captures the full chain of custody: donor, quantity, food type, recipient hub, timestamp.

This distinction matters for CSRD reporting, where traceability from donor to recipient is required. It matters for hub operators, who need advance visibility of incoming donations. And it matters for the food itself, which ends up as a meal rather than methane.

Measuring food waste is useful. Measuring it and doing something about it is better.
