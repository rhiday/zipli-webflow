Why Zipli

Food waste stopped being a moral question in 2026

CSRD ESRS E5 made food waste reporting mandatory for large operators, and the EU Waste Framework Directive set a 30% cut by 31 December 2030. Both need a number. Most operators still produce one by estimating.

Book a demo →

The problem

The regulation arrived before the tooling did

The obligation to report is in force now. The systems that would produce a defensible number are not in place, on either side of the handover.

In the kitchen, the number is a guess

Serving waste is rarely weighed, so the figure that reaches an ESG report is reconstructed after the fact from purchase data and assumptions. An estimate is defensible right up until somebody audits it.

At the terminal, the record is a spreadsheet

Only 2 of 11 Finnish redistribution hubs run any coordination software. 43.5% of all hub hours are volunteer time, and a share of it goes on tracking donations by hand instead of running the terminal.

Why teams choose Zipli

Four reasons, in the order they usually matter

01

Logging that survives a busy shift

One photo. The app reads the scale, recognises the dish and fills in name, category, weight and serving date. If logging costs more than a few seconds, it stops happening by week three, and then there is no data at all.

02

A live view for whoever is coordinating

Batches, messages and container requests appear the moment a kitchen logs them, so a coordinator is reacting to a screen rather than chasing a phone call.

03

Reporting as a by-product, not a project

CSRD and ESRS E5 exports are assembled as the shifts happen. Nobody rebuilds the year in a spreadsheet in January, because there is nothing left to rebuild.

04

The terminal network is the part nobody else has

Measurement tools stop at the kitchen door. Zipli carries the surplus through the handover to a terminal and on to a recipient, so the same event that produces the report also produces a meal.
