Our story

Zipli didn’t start as a food waste company. It started with a waitressing job.

Book a demo →

In her early twenties, Ninja Fedy financed her business studies by working as a temp-help waitress in big restaurants and catering halls. Every time service ended, there were huge piles of untouched food left over that she would have to throw away. That image stayed with her.

“In my early twenties, I financed my business studies by working as a temp-help waitress in big restaurants and catering. Every time service ended, there were huge piles of untouched food left over that I would have to throw away.”

Ninja Fedy

From Lisbon to a pitch competition

In the autumn of 2023, Ninja decided to stop postponing the idea of becoming what she called an “impact entrepreneur” and moved to Lisbon to find out what that could look like. She joined the PUSH accelerator at Casa do Impacto, with Hasan Shahriar already on the team as developer, and pitched LoopTrack, a consumer app comparing the durability and resale value of textiles. Ninja won the pitch competition.

Photo: Ninja and Hasan in Portugal, during the early LoopTrack chapter.

“I was truly surprised when the name of my project was announced after all the great pitches had been given. Without the program I maybe would even not have started to make my idea true yet, and would still be waiting for ‘the right time and place’!”

Ninja Fedy

Photo: the early team in Portugal.

From LoopTrack to Zipli

Over the following months, the idea evolved. Working under the name OnoGrow, the team turned its attention to surplus food, initially with a focus on Southern Europe. In parallel, a connection formed in Finland: the City of Helsinki’s Food Waste Ecosystem project, also involving Vantaa, Luke and Laurea, was piloting food donation to community kitchens but was held back by a lack of digital tools. That gap is what Zipli was built to close.

The company was registered as NoWaste Futures Oy in December 2024, based in Helsinki. Its purpose, as stated plainly in its founding documents: professional kitchens and other large food businesses generate enormous amounts of surplus food every day, and logging, distributing and reporting on it is currently slow, manual and error-prone. Zipli exists to fix that.

Photo: Ninja and Hasan in Helsinki, home base for Zipli.

Two founders, the second time around

Ninja and Hasan first met about eight years ago through mutual friends, not long after Hasan’s internship at Nordea. Years later, the two found themselves working together again on another food-tech startup, Hasan as co-founder and Ninja as an advisor, and that is where she saw firsthand what he could build. When that venture came to an end, she knew exactly who she wanted by her side for the next one: Hasan came on board as Zipli’s co-founder and CTO, and the two have had a lot of fun building it together ever since.

Since Helsinki, Zipli has piloted with the City of Helsinki, Sodexo and Etappi ry, and became the first paying platform for Stadin Safka, Helsinki’s city food redistribution terminal, all while raising over €140,000 in EU Horizon and ELY Centre funding to build the product.

Mission & vision

What we’re building toward

Mission

To ensure that perfectly good food feeds people, not landfills, by making surplus food management effortless, data-rich and commercially viable for businesses.

Vision

By 2030, Zipli is the digital backbone connecting food surplus creators with redistribution systems across Europe, making large-scale food waste prevention the default, not the exception.

Why now

Food waste is a regulatory issue now, not just a moral one

The EU has set a target to cut foodservice food waste by 30% by 2030. New sustainability reporting rules (CSRD/ESRS E5) are requiring large companies to report waste data many of them don’t currently have. France, Spain and other countries already have binding national laws in force. Kitchens and redistribution hubs need a way to capture this data quickly and accurately, without new hardware or added workload. That is what Zipli is built for.

What we stand for

Zipli is a circular economy operating system, not a food delivery company. We solve the discovery, data and compliance problem, and leave physical logistics to existing operators.

“What you don’t measure, you can’t manage.” Real-time data is core to how we think about the problem.

We treat everyone as a possible donor and a possible recipient. Roles stay flexible, not fixed.

Donations to community kitchens are always prioritized, even where a small fee applies to surplus food that’s sold.

As a female-led team, we built our first product on our first €100k grant before asking investors for more.
